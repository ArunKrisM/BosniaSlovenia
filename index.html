What this avoids

Cross-border permit (€65 to €75), the Green Card, the mandatory Zero Excess several Bosnian suppliers impose for taking a car abroad (€15 to €25 a day), and the international one-way drop fee. Together those were the single biggest unknown in the budget.

Also avoided

The Bosnia to Croatia land border, which in peak August is the least predictable part of that drive.

Two domestic hires and a short internal flight, instead of one car crossing two borders.

If you do drive, Rastoke at Slunj is the better stop for a tired four-year-old. A village built over waterfalls, 1 to 2 hours rather than a full day, €9 family ticket, far quieter, and 30 minutes closer to Ljubljana.

What you would gain

Travelling without a pushchair

The good news

Most of the climbing on this trip is done by machine. Cable car at Trebević, funicular in Ljubljana, chairlift at Straža, cable car plus a second chairlift at Vogel, horse carriage at Vrelo Bosne, pletna at Bled, and the train inside Postojna.

The five that need thought

Vrelo Bosne (take the carriage), the Yellow Fortress (steep, or drive to Zmajevac instead), the Bled lakeshore (6km round, so do a stretch), Savica (20 to 30min of steps), and Postojna (~1.5km on foot underground).

Carrier over pushchair anyway

Baščaršija, Počitelj and the Old Town in Mostar are all cobbled or stepped. A carrier would have beaten wheels in most of Bosnia regardless.

What got dropped for this

Martuljek waterfalls, Ojstrica and Mala Osojnica at Bled, Skakavec near Mostar, and Slemenova Špica. All of them an hour or more of carrying.

Before you go

Book both cars early

Book both hires early and confirm in writing that neither needs cross-border cover, since that is where the savings live.

Reserve the Jajce night

Jajce is a small town with limited stock in August, so book that night early.

Pre-book timed entries

Postojna Cave and Vintgar Gorge both need timed tickets online in peak season.

Pack layers

Herzegovina in the south (Mostar, Blagaj, Kravica) runs noticeably hotter than Sarajevo in the mountains. Same day, quite different temperatures.

Two words go a long way

"Zdravo" (hello) and "hvala" (thank you) are reliably well received. Locals are widely described as warm and welcoming.

Cash for the small stuff

Bosnia uses the convertible mark (KM). Museums, parking attendants and the older bakeries and cafés are frequently cash-only.

Straža toboggan, tandem age

Worth confirming the tandem-sledge minimum age on the day, though it's generally fine for a 4-year-old riding with an adult.
